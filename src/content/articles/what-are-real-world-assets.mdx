Real-world assets, shortened to RWAs, refer to tangible or traditionally-structured financial assets — government debt, corporate bonds, real estate, private credit, commodities, invoices — that are represented onchain through a token.

An RWA token is not the asset itself. It's a digital claim, typically backed by a legal agreement, that entitles the holder to some right associated with the underlying asset: a share of income, a redemption right, or beneficial ownership. The legal structure connecting the token to the asset — a trust, a special purpose vehicle, a fund share class — is what makes the claim enforceable.

## Common RWA categories

Tokenized government debt, especially short-duration U.S. Treasuries, has become the largest and most liquid RWA category, used by both institutions and onchain protocols as a yield-bearing cash equivalent. Private credit — direct lending tokenized into onchain vehicles — has grown quickly as an alternative to bank lending. Real estate, trade finance, commodities and carbon credits make up smaller but growing segments.

## What RWAs are not

RWAs are distinct from native crypto assets like ether or bitcoin, which have no off-chain legal claim behind them, and distinct from purely synthetic instruments that reference a price without any underlying legal ownership. The defining feature of an RWA is the legal bridge back to something that exists off-chain.
