Stablecoins and tokenized deposits both aim to let dollars move with the speed and programmability of a blockchain, but they represent fundamentally different legal claims.

## Stablecoins

A stablecoin is typically issued by a non-bank entity and represents a claim on a pool of reserves — cash and short-term instruments — held by the issuer. It is not a bank deposit and, in most jurisdictions, is not covered by deposit insurance. Its value depends on the issuer's solvency and the quality of its reserve management.

## Tokenized deposits

A tokenized deposit is a digital representation of an actual deposit at a regulated bank. It is a direct liability of that bank, generally covered by the same deposit insurance and regulatory protections as a conventional bank account. Several large banks have piloted tokenized deposit programs aimed at enabling near-instant settlement between institutional clients on the same platform.

## The practical trade-off

Stablecoins tend to be more portable — usable across many platforms, protocols and blockchains without needing a relationship with a specific bank. Tokenized deposits offer stronger regulatory protections but are generally limited to the issuing bank's network and client base, at least in current pilot programs. Which is preferable depends heavily on whether portability or institutional-grade protection matters more for a given use case.
