Singapore's Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) announced on 19 November that it had issued S$100 million of tokenised MAS Bills on 6 November 2026. The four-week instruments were created directly on the central bank's Singapore Dollar Test Network and settled using wholesale central bank digital currency (CBDC), according to the authority's release. They are scheduled to mature on 4 December.

Five approved primary dealers participated in the auction: DBS, HSBC's Singapore branch, OCBC, Standard Chartered Bank Singapore and UOB. MAS said total applications exceeded twice the issuance amount. Unlike securities issued through conventional systems and tokenised afterward, these bills were recorded natively on the shared ledger, reducing the need to reconcile separate records.

MAS intends to rename the Singapore Dollar Test Network the SG Digital Ledger. It plans further live trials in 2027, with more participating institutions and an expanded role for tokenised bills in collateral management. The announcement describes an institutional market-infrastructure experiment, not a retail product launch or a completed nationwide replacement for traditional settlement.
