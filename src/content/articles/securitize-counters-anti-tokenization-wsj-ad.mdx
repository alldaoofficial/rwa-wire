A policy fight over tokenized stocks moved into paid newspaper advertising. CoinDesk reported that the Common Sense Leadership Fund placed a full-page warning in The Wall Street Journal's October 24-25 weekend edition. The ad addressed public-company CEOs, described third-party tokenization as a threat and urged them to act. CoinDesk said the nonprofit's ultimate campaign financing was not disclosed.

Securitize answered with its own full-page Journal ad on October 28. The company presented a different model: tokens backed one-for-one by actual shares, carrying voting and dividend rights, offered through an SEC-registered broker-dealer to verified investors. Those are Securitize's claims about its structure, not a blanket description of every tokenized stock product.

The regulatory distinction matters. The SEC's September 17 order is temporary and conditional. For stock tokenized by an unaffiliated third party, a covered venue must provide the issuer written notice and an opportunity to object. CoinDesk also reported that the first ad linked to a Nasdaq FAQ, while Nasdaq denied involvement with the campaign. The advertising exchange is a new event, separate from the previously reported OKXICE issuer objections, and does not establish that either ad caused any issuer decision.
