A **tokenized stock** is a blockchain-based representation of a security or of an economic claim linked to a security. That definition matters because products carrying the same company name can be structured very differently.

The first question should therefore never be only “Which stock does this track?” It should be **“What exactly does this token legally represent?”**

## Three structures you should understand

### Issuer-sponsored tokenization

The company or its authorized infrastructure can issue or record the security using distributed-ledger technology. In this structure, the blockchain record can form part of the official ownership system.

### Custodial or wrapped exposure

A third party holds traditional shares with a custodian and issues tokens representing an entitlement connected to those shares. The investor may therefore hold a claim through an intermediary rather than appearing as the direct registered shareholder.

### Synthetic exposure

A token can provide economic exposure to a stock price without conveying ownership of the underlying share. This introduces a different legal structure and different counterparty risks.

The visual experience may be similar — a token in an account or wallet — while the legal substance is not.

## What rights can differ?

Depending on the structure, holders should check whether they receive:

- dividends or equivalent distributions;
- voting rights;
- corporate-action treatment;
- redemption into traditional shares or cash;
- bankruptcy protections;
- direct or indirect ownership of the underlying security.

A token tracking a stock price is not enough information to answer those questions.

## Why tokenize stocks?

Potential advantages include programmable settlement, integration with digital collateral systems, fractional units and the ability to connect securities with blockchain-based financial infrastructure.

Tokenization may reduce some operational boundaries between an asset and the systems using that asset for collateral or settlement. Whether those advantages are realized depends on regulation, liquidity, custody and interoperability — not merely the existence of a token.

## Are tokenized stocks still securities?

In the United States, SEC staff has stated that a financial instrument does not stop being a security simply because it is represented as a crypto asset. The structure and rights remain central to the regulatory analysis.

That distinction is useful more broadly: **technology changes representation and market infrastructure; it does not automatically change the economic substance of an instrument.**

## The tokenized-stock checklist

Before evaluating a tokenized equity product, identify:

**Issuer:** Who created the token?  
**Underlying:** Is an actual share held somewhere?  
**Custodian:** Who controls it?  
**Rights:** Do token holders receive dividends, votes or other shareholder rights?  
**Redemption:** Can the token be converted or redeemed?  
**Record:** Which ledger is legally authoritative?  
**Jurisdiction:** Where may the product legally be offered and traded?

## Why market structure matters

The long-term importance of tokenized equities is larger than extended trading hours or fractional shares. The bigger question is whether securities can become usable components of digital financial infrastructure — moving between regulated venues, settlement systems and collateral workflows without fragmenting investor rights.
