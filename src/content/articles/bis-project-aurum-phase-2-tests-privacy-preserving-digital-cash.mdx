The BIS Innovation Hub published Project Aurum Phase 2 on October 13. The research prototype builds on Aurum's two-tier digital-cash architecture and adds privacy-enhancing technologies at the retail layer. It combines encryption, BBS+ signatures, pseudonym-based ownership and zero-knowledge proofs with configurable compliance mechanisms.

BIS presents the work as an exploratory proof of concept, not a deployed central bank digital currency or public payment service. The project examines a middle ground between complete anonymity and full transaction transparency. Its design separates responsibilities and data access while retaining payment-integrity controls.

The report also identifies trade-offs: stronger privacy may affect programmability, performance and operational complexity. The result is therefore a technical reference for further experimentation and policy discussion, not evidence that a central bank has approved or launched this architecture.
