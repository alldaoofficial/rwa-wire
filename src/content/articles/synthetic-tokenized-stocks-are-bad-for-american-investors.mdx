## An industry argument

Prometheum published a critique of synthetic tokenized stocks, arguing that investors should pay attention to the rights behind stock-linked instruments. The company is a commercial participant in digital-asset securities markets. Its position should be read as an argument, rather than a neutral finding that every synthetic product is unsuitable.

## The official distinction

Investor.gov describes issuer-sponsored, custodial and synthetic forms of tokenized securities. A synthetic instrument can provide a security-linked exposure without being the underlying issuer's share. It can itself still be a security.

## What the comparison should examine

The useful comparison is the holder's actual claim: who owes the obligation, which rights attach to it, and how the position can be redeemed or transferred. A familiar stock ticker does not answer those questions.

This does not establish that one structure is appropriate for every investor. It establishes why product descriptions should identify ownership rights and price exposure separately, rather than use “tokenized stock” as if it guaranteed the same instrument in every case.
