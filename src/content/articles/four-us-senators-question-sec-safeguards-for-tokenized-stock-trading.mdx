Four US senators have asked Securities and Exchange Commission Chair Paul Atkins to clarify safeguards governing tokenized stock trading under the agency's September 17 Innovation Exemption. In a letter published by Senator Angela Alsobrooks on November 16, she joined Catherine Cortez Masto, Mark Warner and Ruben Gallego in raising concerns about illicit-finance controls, issuer consent and retail investor protection.

The lawmakers argue that permissioned access to a trading venue does not necessarily prevent a tokenized share from later reaching an unverified or offshore wallet. They also question whether the framework establishes clear anti-money-laundering obligations for venues that are not registered banks or broker-dealers.

On issuer rights, the senators highlight a 30-day objection window and say more than 60 public companies have objected to tokenization of their shares. Their letter also asks how best execution, custody and investor ownership rights would work when retail customers trade directly on tokenized securities venues.

The senators submitted 13 questions and requested answers within 30 days. Their letter calls for transparent rulemaking rather than treating the temporary exemption as a settled regulatory model. These are concerns raised by lawmakers, not findings that platforms have committed violations.
