A tokenized fund is a traditional pooled investment vehicle — a money market fund, a private credit fund, occasionally a broader strategy fund — that issues one of its share classes as a blockchain-based token rather than, or in addition to, a conventional book-entry share.

## What changes, and what doesn't

The fund's investment strategy, manager obligations, fee structure and regulatory registration are unaffected by tokenization. What changes is the record-keeping and transfer mechanism for that particular share class: instead of a transfer agent updating a traditional register, ownership is recorded and moved on a blockchain, often with the transfer agent function still involved in an oversight or reconciliation role.

## Where adoption has concentrated

Money market funds have been the most visible category, largely because their stable net asset value and daily liquidity make them a natural fit for onchain cash-management use cases. Private credit funds have also tokenized share classes, aiming to broaden their distribution and, in some cases, allow tokens to be used as collateral in other onchain transactions.

## Investor considerations

Because the underlying fund structure is unchanged, the same due diligence that applies to any fund — manager track record, fee levels, redemption terms, liquidity risk — applies equally to its tokenized share class. The token wrapper does not substitute for reading the fund's actual offering documents.
