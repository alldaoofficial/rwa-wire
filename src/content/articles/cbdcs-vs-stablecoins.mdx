Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) and stablecoins are sometimes discussed interchangeably, but they sit on opposite sides of the public-private divide in money.

## Who issues the claim

A CBDC is issued directly by a country's central bank and represents a direct liability of that central bank — the same category of claim as physical cash, just in digital form. A stablecoin is issued by a private company and represents a claim on that company's reserves, carrying credit and operational risk that a central bank liability does not.

## Retail vs wholesale designs

Central banks researching or piloting CBDCs generally explore two models. A retail CBDC would be available directly to the public for everyday payments, raising questions about the role commercial banks play as intermediaries. A wholesale CBDC would be restricted to banks and other financial institutions for interbank settlement, closer in spirit to how central bank reserves already function, just represented on newer settlement infrastructure.

## Why the distinction matters for tokenized finance

Some tokenization programs are designed to eventually settle against a CBDC rather than a private stablecoin, which would remove reserve and counterparty risk from the settlement leg of a transaction. Progress varies significantly by country, and several major economies remain in research or pilot phases rather than live deployment.
