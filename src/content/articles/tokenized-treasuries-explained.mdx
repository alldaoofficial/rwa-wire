Tokenized Treasuries are digital tokens representing shares in a fund that holds short-duration U.S. government debt — typically Treasury bills with maturities under a year. The fund structure is what makes the token a legal claim; the blockchain layer is what makes it transferable and, on some platforms, usable as collateral.

## Why they've grown

Onchain participants — from trading desks to decentralized finance protocols — often hold significant balances in stablecoins, which by design earn no yield for the holder. Tokenized Treasuries offer a way to hold a similar, dollar-denominated, low-volatility instrument while earning a yield tied to prevailing short-term rates. That combination has made them one of the fastest-growing RWA categories by assets under management.

## How they typically work

An investor sends stablecoins or another accepted asset to the issuer, who uses the proceeds to purchase Treasuries inside the fund. The investor receives tokens representing their share of the fund. As the underlying Treasuries earn interest, that is reflected either through a growing token balance, a rising token price, or periodic distributions, depending on the issuer's structure.

## What varies between issuers

Not all tokenized Treasury products are alike. Redemption speed, minimum investment size, eligible investor categories (some are restricted to qualified or institutional investors), and which blockchains a token is available on all differ by issuer. Reading the fund's offering documents remains essential — the token wrapper does not change the underlying legal terms.
