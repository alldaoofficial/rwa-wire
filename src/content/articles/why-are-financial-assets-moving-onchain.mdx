Tokenization has moved from an experimental blockchain use case to a stated infrastructure priority for a growing list of banks, asset managers and exchanges. Three motivations come up repeatedly in public statements from institutions running live programs.

## Settlement efficiency

Traditional securities settlement — particularly across borders — still relies on multiple intermediaries reconciling separate ledgers, a process that can take one to several business days. Tokenized instruments settle on a shared ledger, cutting settlement time and the operational risk that comes with it.

## Collateral mobility

Tokenized money market funds and Treasuries can be used as collateral and moved between counterparties far faster than assets held in traditional custodial accounts. For trading desks that need to post and recall collateral throughout the day, this speed has direct funding-cost implications.

## Distribution reach

A tokenized fund share can, in principle, be distributed to a broader and more programmable set of platforms and wallets than a traditional share class registered through conventional transfer agents — though this depends heavily on the regulatory permissions attached to each program.

## Where adoption is concentrated

Most live institutional deployments today involve cash-equivalents — tokenized Treasuries and money market funds — used primarily as collateral or short-duration cash management tools. More complex asset classes, such as private equity or structured credit, are earlier in their tokenization roadmaps and typically involve smaller pilot programs.
