Bond tokenization applies the same core idea as other RWA categories — representing a legal debt obligation as a transferable digital token — to fixed income instruments ranging from sovereign and supranational debt to corporate bonds.

## Why bonds were an early tokenization target

Bonds have well-defined cash flows, fixed maturities and standardized documentation, which makes them relatively straightforward to encode into smart contracts compared with more complex instruments. Several governments and supranational institutions have issued tokenized or "digital native" bonds as pilot programs, often run in parallel with a traditional issuance to compare processes directly.

## Cited benefits

Issuers and underwriters running these pilots have pointed to faster settlement — some programs report same-day or near-instant settlement compared with the standard multi-day cycle — reduced administrative overhead from automated coupon payments, and the potential to reach a wider base of investors through digital distribution channels.

## Current limitations

Secondary trading of tokenized bonds remains thinner than trading in established bond markets, meaning investors may find it harder to sell before maturity at a competitive price. Legal and regulatory frameworks for digital bond issuance also continue to vary by jurisdiction, which shapes which investors can participate and on which platforms.
