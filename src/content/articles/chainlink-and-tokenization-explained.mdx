Chainlink is infrastructure that connects blockchains to data and systems that exist outside them — a function commonly referred to as an "oracle." For tokenized assets, this connective role has become directly relevant to how those assets are priced, verified and moved.

## The oracle function

Smart contracts cannot natively access information from outside their own blockchain. Chainlink's network of independent node operators retrieves external data — market prices, proof-of-reserve attestations, interest rate benchmarks — and delivers it onchain in a form smart contracts can use. For a tokenized fund, this might mean automatically verifying that the fund's onchain token supply matches its actual off-chain reserves.

## Cross-chain interoperability

Chainlink's Cross-Chain Interoperability Protocol (CCIP) is designed to let tokens, data and instructions move between different blockchain networks in a standardized way. As tokenization programs increasingly span multiple blockchains rather than a single one, this cross-chain layer has become a component several institutional pilots reference directly.

## Institutional involvement

A number of banks and asset managers running tokenization proofs-of-concept have used Chainlink infrastructure for tasks like cross-chain settlement messaging or data verification, alongside its established use across decentralized finance protocols.

This profile describes Chainlink's technical role in the tokenization landscape. It is not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold its associated token.
