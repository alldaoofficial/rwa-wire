Higher yields on short-dated US government debt are renewing questions about the opportunity cost of holding non-interest-bearing stablecoins. In a November 21 analysis, CCN reported analyst Jamie Coutts' estimate of a negative 0.62 correlation between changes in stablecoin supply and Treasury bill yields since 2022. That historical association does not establish that investors are switching directly into tokenized funds.\n\nCCN also cited a DefiLlama study published November 12. The study examined 98 large stablecoin pools without token incentives, reporting a median base yield of 4.07%, compared with a 4.17% three-month Treasury bill benchmark measured earlier in November. Forty-eight pools exceeded that benchmark, although higher returns can carry additional credit, smart-contract and liquidity risks.\n\nSeparate November 17 reporting by Bloomingbit described Coutts' view that stablecoin supply had fallen by $17 billion since August, with funds leaving the crypto market rather than rotating into tokenized money market products. Tokenized funds can offer exposure to Treasury income, but subscription thresholds, investor restrictions and redemption windows can prevent them from serving as immediate substitutes for liquid payment tokens. The potential migration remains a hypothesis, not a documented inflow.
