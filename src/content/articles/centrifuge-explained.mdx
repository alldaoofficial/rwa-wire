Centrifuge is infrastructure for **onchain asset management**. Managers can use it to operate tokenized financial products, including funds and private-credit offerings. It is broader than a marketplace for loans: its manager documentation also covers Treasury, money-market, equity and onchain strategies.

## How Centrifuge works

A manager structures a financial product and configures its onchain operations. These can include investor access, share issuance, pricing, subscriptions and reporting. The onchain setup needs to reflect the product's underlying structure; deploying a token does not establish its legal terms by itself.

Three concepts help explain the investor-facing workflow:

| Component | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Pool | Organizes a financial product's accounting, share classes and management permissions. |
| Share token | Represents a claim associated with the pool; the particular product determines its terms. |
| Vault | Provides an entry point for investing in or requesting redemption from a share class, using a specified asset and network. |

A pool can coordinate activity across networks. That does not mean every share token is freely transferable: investor permissions and the product's rules still apply.

For the broader structure, read [how tokenized funds work](/rwa/tokenized-funds-explained/).

## Are deposits and withdrawals instant?

Not necessarily. Centrifuge documents two vault models:

- **Synchronous vaults:** a deposit can return shares in the same transaction, but redemptions remain request-based.
- **Asynchronous vaults:** investors request deposits or redemptions, the manager processes the orders, and investors then claim the resulting shares or assets.

Request-based processing can accommodate periodic valuations and offchain settlement. A token being transferable on a network therefore does not establish that a fund can redeem it immediately for cash.

## Is CFG the same as a fund-share token?

No. **CFG is Centrifuge's ecosystem token**, documented as an ERC-20 token on Ethereum. A share token belongs to a particular financial product. Buying CFG is not the same transaction as subscribing to one of those products and does not, by itself, give the buyer its fund shares.

## What should readers check for each product?

The infrastructure is only one part of the assessment. Read the product's own documentation to establish:

1. **Exposure:** which assets or strategy generate its returns, and who manages them?
2. **Rights and access:** what claim does a share represent, and who is eligible to hold or redeem it?
3. **Liquidity:** when are requests processed, and what conditions can delay payment?
4. **Dependencies:** which custody, valuation, contract and operational arrangements does the product rely on?

Private credit adds borrower and underwriting risk; a Treasury-oriented product has a different exposure. Neither the Centrifuge name nor the presence of a token is a substitute for examining the individual offering.
