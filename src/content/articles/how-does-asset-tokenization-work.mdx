Tokenizing an asset generally involves four layers working together: a legal structure, an issuance platform, a blockchain settlement layer, and ongoing servicing.

## 1. Legal structuring

Before anything is written to a blockchain, the asset is placed into a legal vehicle — a fund, trust or special purpose vehicle — that defines who owns what and what rights token holders actually have. This step is what makes the token an enforceable claim rather than a purely digital artifact.

## 2. Issuance

An issuance platform mints tokens representing shares or units in that legal vehicle. This step typically includes KYC/AML checks on investors, jurisdictional eligibility rules, and minimum investment thresholds, all encoded so that only eligible wallets can hold or receive the token.

## 3. Settlement

Once issued, tokens can be transferred and settled on a blockchain. Transfers that would traditionally take one or more business days to clear through intermediaries can settle in minutes, because ownership records update directly rather than through a chain of reconciled ledgers.

## 4. Servicing

After issuance, the asset still needs ongoing administration: interest or dividend distributions, redemptions, reporting and corporate actions. Increasingly, this servicing is automated through smart contracts, though many programs still rely on a traditional transfer agent or administrator operating alongside the token.

Most institutional tokenization programs today run on permissioned or hybrid infrastructure — blockchains or blockchain modules with built-in access controls — rather than fully open public networks, reflecting the compliance requirements attached to regulated securities.
