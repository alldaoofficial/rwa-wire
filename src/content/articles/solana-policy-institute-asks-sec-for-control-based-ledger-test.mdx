The Solana Policy Institute asked the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission to clarify what qualifies as a public, permissionless distributed ledger for tokenized securities venues. Its October 22 comment supports a functional, control-based standard rather than a prescribed network architecture.

The group proposed five considerations: open-source code; open reading and transaction submission; execution under pre-established transparent rules; whether one person or controlled group has persistent authority over sequencing, validation, inclusion, finality or consensus changes; and whether anyone can unilaterally censor use or receive preferential permissions.

The letter also says temporary, disclosed incident-response measures should not automatically disqualify a network. These are recommendations from an industry policy group, not SEC guidance, an approval or a change to the exemption.
