The stablecoin sector has continued to see active development across regulation, issuance and distribution over recent months, reflecting its growing role as settlement infrastructure for tokenized markets more broadly.

## Regulatory developments

Multiple jurisdictions have continued refining frameworks that define reserve requirements, disclosure obligations and licensing pathways for stablecoin issuers. The general direction across major markets has been toward clearer rules rather than prohibition, though specific requirements — particularly around reserve composition and redemption guarantees — continue to differ meaningfully by jurisdiction.

## Competitive dynamics

Among leading stablecoin issuers, competition has increasingly centered on reserve transparency — the frequency and rigor of attestations or audits covering backing assets — and on distribution partnerships that embed stablecoins directly into payment platforms, exchanges and, increasingly, tokenization platforms that use them as the settlement asset for RWA transactions.

## Bank entrants

Several banks have continued to explore or expand tokenized deposit and stablecoin-adjacent initiatives, aimed primarily at institutional clients needing faster settlement for treasury and collateral operations. These programs remain distinct from public, freely transferable stablecoins in both structure and intended user base.

## Why this matters for tokenized markets

Because stablecoins function as the primary cash leg in most tokenized asset transactions, shifts in their regulatory footing and competitive landscape have direct implications for how tokenized RWA markets settle and scale.
