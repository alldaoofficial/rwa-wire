A stablecoin is a blockchain-based token designed to maintain a stable value relative to a reference asset, most commonly the U.S. dollar. Unlike volatile crypto assets, a stablecoin's core design goal is price stability, which makes it useful as a medium of exchange and a settlement asset within onchain markets.

## The dominant model: fiat-backed reserves

The largest stablecoins by market capitalization are backed by reserves of cash and short-duration, high-quality assets like Treasury bills, held by the issuer and, in principle, redeemable one-for-one for the pegged currency. The credibility of this model rests on the quality of the reserves and the transparency of how they're reported and audited.

## Other models

Some stablecoins use over-collateralized crypto reserves, locking a larger value of volatile crypto assets to back a smaller stablecoin issuance, with automated mechanisms to manage that collateral ratio. Algorithmic models that rely primarily on market incentives rather than collateral have proven considerably more fragile historically and represent a much smaller share of the market today.

## Why they matter to tokenized finance

Stablecoins function as the primary settlement asset across most tokenized markets — the "cash leg" used to buy and sell tokenized Treasuries, bonds and funds. Their reliability directly affects the reliability of the broader onchain financial system built on top of them.
